## Yoyu v1.15.0

**A third board at about $20**, and a theme that changes how your headroom is
drawn rather than only what colour it is.

### A cheaper board: the 1.47" C6

[Waveshare's ESP32-C6-LCD-1.47](https://www.waveshare.com/esp32-c6-lcd-1.47.htm)
is 172×320 and around $20, which makes it the cheapest way to own one of these.
It is a different chip family from the other two — RISC-V, single core, no
PSRAM, 4MB of flash — so it carries its own partition table and its own
firmware image, and the setup page picks the right one for you.

A panel that narrow is no place for nine screens, so it opens on two: **Micro**,
which is what is left drawn as large as the glass allows, and **Pace**, which is
whether you will run out before the window resets. It also sits sideways: 320×172
across a desk reads far better than 172×320 standing up, and there is an
**Orientation** setting for it on every board.

There is a colour LED under the acrylic. It tracks your headroom without you
having to read anything — green above 50%, amber above 10%, red below.

No touch on this board at all. There is no touch layer to fail, so the screens
change on a timer and everything else is set from a browser.

### A theme is now a layout, not just a palette

There were six themes and they were six colour schemes over one layout, which
meant the screen you stare at most looked the same in all six.

There are **thirteen** now, over **ten layouts**. Corner brackets and a
segmented gauge. Terminal lines under a blinking cursor. Laboratory columns
filling from the bottom. Slanted bands of light behind offset type. A printed
docket with a stamp. A 1989 handheld with hearts instead of a percentage. Nord
and Tokyo Night keep the plain layout, because a palette swap is a legitimate
theme and those two always were one.

The layout applies to **Meters, Focus and Micro** — the three screens that show
your headroom. Each keeps what makes it itself: Meters keeps the clock, the
credits row and the freshness footer, Focus still picks the window you are
closest to running out of and keeps its projection, Micro gives the whole panel
over to the numbers.

Both places you choose a theme now show you all thirteen. The board's own
settings page draws a miniature of each layout on a card, and the setup wizard
shows the same thirteen drawings and carries your choice over to the board.

Your current theme is kept. The first six themes have the numbers they always
had, and the new ones are appended after them.

### Running out no longer takes over the screen

A window at zero used to replace whatever you were looking at with a
full-screen countdown to the reset. That was the right answer when there was
one thing to say about being out; it is the wrong one now that the screen you
are on already says it.

Zero is a number like any other now, drawn in your theme's layout, and it
**pulses** while it is critical rather than commandeering the display.

### A tenth screen: Micro

Meters spends its top third on a clock and a plan name. Micro is the same
information with all of that taken away — the windows that matter, as large as
the panel allows, drawn in your theme's layout. It is meant for reading across
a room, and it is the default on the C6.

It is switched on once, the way every screen added since v1.4.0 has been, and
stays off if you turn it off.

### A board nobody can tap can no longer be stranded

Auto-rotate set to **Off (tap only)** means "I will change the screen myself."
On a board with no working touch it means a screen with no way off it — and the
board's own settings page offered that option anyway. A board could be left on
Meters with nine other screens it would never show again.

Boards that cannot be tapped no longer offer it, an "off" arriving from an old
saved setting is turned into 20 seconds, and a board already stuck this way
frees itself on its next restart. Pinning a **single** screen is still allowed,
because with one screen there is nowhere to rotate to and that is a choice
rather than a trap.

### For checking

`/api/status` gains six fields, all of them there because something was hard to
diagnose without them:

- `reset_reason` and `reset_code` — why the board last started: a power-on, a
  brownout, a crash, a watchdog, or its own restart. A board that reboots and
  comes back with older settings looks like it undid your changes; this is what
  tells the two apart.
- `uptime_s` — seconds since that reset, which is what makes a restart visible
  between two checks without having to catch the board while it is down.
- `touch_ok`, `touch_input` and `touch_events` — whether the touch controller
  initialised, whether presses from it are acted on, and how many it has
  reported. A board whose touch is known not to work should sit at zero.

### Smaller things

- **The setup page was offering nine of ten screens.** Micro was never added to
  its picker.
- **Improv told the browser flasher `ESP32-S3`** whatever board it was running
  on.
- **Text on a narrow panel could split in two.** The measurement used to size
  text measured the *wrapped* box, so a string 234 pixels wide was told it fit
  in 172 and printed at full size across two lines.
- **The sideways setting reverted on every reboot**, because it was applied
  before saved settings were loaded.

### Upgrading

Over the air from `/update`, or re-flash from the setup page. Wi-Fi, login,
settings and history all survive.

C6 owners: there is no earlier firmware to update from, so flash it from the
setup page.
