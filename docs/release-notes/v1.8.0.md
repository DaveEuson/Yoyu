## Yoyu v1.8.0

**Five characters instead of one, a way to disconnect that actually
disconnects, and a battery gauge on the AMOLED board.**

### Pick a character

The kitsune is no longer the only one. `/settings` now offers five, and each is
a **gauge rather than a decoration** — every one of them says how much headroom
is left, in its own way:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Kitsune** | fans out one to three tails |
| **Moon** | waxes through its phase |
| **Candle** | burns down |
| **Plant** | grows, a leaf pair at a time |
| **Cat** | its ball of yarn gets bigger |

Four of the five are **continuous**, where the tails are three buckets. A moon
at 47% looks like 47% instead of rounding into "middling" — which is the actual
reason to offer a choice rather than just more pictures. The picker names the
gauge next to the name for the same reason.

The four new ones share a cell grid that feeds both surfaces: the panel paints
it, and the board's own page emits the same thing as SVG. So the glass and the
page cannot drift apart. The kitsune keeps its own renderer — it has sub-cell
mood features nothing else needs.

Characters keep fixed colours across all six themes, as the kitsune always did.
One that changed colour with the theme would stop being the same character.

### Disconnecting is as easy as connecting

Pairing a board takes one click. Disconnecting meant knowing that `/connect`
existed — a page whose own first line says it is for people who *cannot* run
the companion — and finding a grey button at the bottom of it. There was
nothing in the tray at all.

Now there is a **Claude account** section on `/settings`, behind a confirmation,
saying plainly what goes and what stays: the login and the top-up key go; Wi-Fi,
theme, character, screens and history stay. The tray grows **Disconnect from
Claude** beside **Pair this board**, per board where you run two. From a
terminal, `companion.py --disconnect`.

> **A disconnect that did not disconnect.** Underneath this was a real bug. The
> board persists its top-up key in flash, and disconnecting cleared it only in
> RAM — so the next reboot read it straight back. A "disconnected" board came
> back still accepting top-ups from whatever last paired with it, and kept a
> live credential in flash besides. Fixed: the key is erased, and the board now
> answers the old one with a 401.

Both ends forget together. The board revokes its key and the computer drops its
copy, so no dead key is left to produce a confusing refusal later.

### The AMOLED board has a battery gauge

`boards.h` carried *"no battery divider is documented for this board"*, which
turned out to be the wrong question. There is no divider because the board does
not need one: it carries an **AXP2101** power-management chip that already
measures the cell and keeps a fuel gauge. It has been answering at `0x34` on
the I²C bus the whole time.

So the percentage now comes from the gauge itself, rather than from a voltage
curve fitted by hand the way the 2″ board has to.

**`battery` and `charging` are also in `/api/status` now**, for both boards. The
gauge was drawn on the glass and nowhere else, so "is it on battery, and how
flat" could only be answered by walking over and looking at it.

### AMOLED touch: it is the panel

Earlier notes left this "unsolved". It is now settled, and the docs said the
wrong thing about it.

Waveshare's own driver — their `SensorLib`, their pin definitions, no Yoyu code
involved — reports no touch either, and the controller's interrupt line never
fires. Their schematic then rules out the wiring: display and touch share one
24-pin FPC at `J40`, the display works through it, and the touch controller
answers on I²C through it. Neither the CST9220 nor the CO5300 is on the
mainboard schematic at all, so both are bonded to the panel module.

The dead link is between the controller and the sensing grid it sits on, inside
that module. **Do not open the case** — there is nothing in there to reseat. A
board that does this needs replacing.

### Upgrading

Over the air from `/update`, or re-flash from the setup page. Wi-Fi, login,
settings and history all survive.
